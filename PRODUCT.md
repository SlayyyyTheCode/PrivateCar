# Product

## Register

product

The tab screens are the product: calculators, a verdict, data over time. The landing page is a front door to them, not the thing itself.

## Users

Singaporeans deciding whether to buy a specific car, usually with a dealer listing already open in another tab. They are numerate but not financial professionals, and they are being quoted a monthly instalment by someone who benefits from them signing. They are on a phone, often standing in a showroom or sitting with a spreadsheet at home in the evening.

The job: *find out whether I can actually afford this car, before I commit.* Not "explore car finance" — a specific car, a specific salary, a yes or no.

## Product Purpose

Every other number a buyer sees is the instalment. OYC adds the eleven costs the instalment hides — petrol, parking, insurance, ERP, road tax, servicing, the interest a flat rate disguises — and reduces the total to one honest word against their income.

Success is a user putting the phone down and either buying with clear eyes or walking away. Either outcome is a win; a user who is *reassured but wrong* is the failure mode.

## Brand Personality

**Straight, exacting, unflinching.**

The voice of a friend who happens to be an accountant: warm enough to keep reading, unwilling to soften a bad number. It says "Not this car, not yet" rather than "This may be a stretch." It shows its sources and its vintage because it expects to be checked.

Never salesy. Never congratulatory. Never uses a car's glamour to sell the app — the photographs are evidence, not seduction.

## Anti-references

- **Dealer finance calculators.** Glossy hero car, one big instalment figure, a CTA to "Get approved". They exist to make the number look small.
- **Fintech dashboards.** Gradient cards, confetti, "You're doing great!" celebration states. This app frequently delivers bad news and must not flinch.
- **Insurance comparison sites.** Cluttered, badge-laden, every element shouting.
- Anything that reads as *AI-generated*: identical rounded cards stacked forever, an uppercase tracked eyebrow above every section, decorative glass.

## Design Principles

1. **The verdict is the interface.** One number, one word. Everything else is evidence supporting it, and should recede accordingly.
2. **Confidence is earned, and shown.** Every figure carries its source and its date. Where data is indicative rather than official, the interface says so at the same size as the number itself.
3. **Bad news, delivered plainly.** Red is for the truth, not for alarm. No softening, no celebration, no emotional manipulation in either direction.
4. **Density is respect.** These users are reading a lot of numbers. Tight, aligned, tabular — not airy cards with one figure each.
5. **Never a number without a basis.** If it cannot be sourced or derived, it does not appear.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA: body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, verified rather than assumed.
- Status is never carried by colour alone — the verdict is a *word* first, colour second. Charts pair every series with a named legend entry and a text readout.
- Chart palettes are validated for protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, with separate steps for dark mode.
- Respects the system colour scheme. Motion is short and non-essential; nothing is gated behind an animation completing.
- Tap targets ≥44px. Currency uses tabular figures so columns align for scanning.
